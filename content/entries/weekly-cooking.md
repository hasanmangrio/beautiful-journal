There's a version of me that exists only in my own imagination who makes different, interesting things for dinner every night. Who meal plans with spreadsheets. Who renders duck fat and uses it for potatoes. Who has opinions about fish sauce brands.

Then there's the actual version, who on a Tuesday at 7:30pm is looking at a box of pasta and an onion and making something that's technically not a recipe but is also deeply delicious.

I've made peace with the second version.

## The Five Things

Here they are, in no particular order:

**1. Pasta with whatever's in the fridge.** The genre is flexible — aglio e olio if there's only garlic and oil, something creamy if there's leftover ricotta, a vinegary situation with whatever vegetables need using. The technique is always the same: toast something, make fat taste good, add pasta water, finish with cheese. It works every time.

**2. Sheet pan chicken thighs.** Bone-in, skin-on, 425°F, 35 minutes. Season aggressively with whatever's on the spice shelf. Put vegetables in the same pan — potatoes, or fennel, or cherry tomatoes. Don't overthink it.

**3. Dal.** This one takes longer but is almost entirely passive — it simmers while I do other things and the apartment smells incredible for an hour. Red lentils, one can of tomatoes, cumin and turmeric and a bay leaf, finished with a spoonful of butter. I make it on Sundays so I have it for lunch all week.

**4. Eggs in some form.** Scrambled, fried, baked in a pan with tomato sauce, frittata with whatever's around. Eggs are a kitchen reset button. When I don't know what to make, the answer is almost always eggs.

**5. The salad that's actually a meal.** This is the most recent addition. Hearty greens (arugula, kale, whatever), something roasted, something sharp (feta, or pickled onion), something crunchy. Dress it properly — this is the only place I'm exacting — with good olive oil and enough acid and salt.

## Why This Is Fine

I used to feel a vague guilt about the repetition. Like I was failing at some implied promise of domestic creativity. Cookbooks I'd bought and not used sitting on the shelf like an accusation.

But I've started thinking about it differently. The five things are good. They're genuinely, actually good — I've made them enough that I can do them without thinking, which means I can do them when I'm tired, which is when dinner happens. And "food I'll actually make" is more important than "food I might theoretically make if I had more time and energy and a different personality."

Also: there's real pleasure in the repetition. In knowing exactly how the pasta will taste before you make it. In the satisfying familiarity of the same sheet pan, the same temperatures, the same result.

The duck fat version of me doesn't exist. But this version makes a really good dal.

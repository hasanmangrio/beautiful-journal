I moved in on a Saturday in January when it was raining. The movers were efficient and kind and gone by noon, and then I was alone in an empty apartment with a box cutter and an overwhelming desire to go back to my old place.

That feeling lasted about six weeks.

It's not that anything was wrong. The apartment is objectively better — more light, newer kitchen, quiet street. But it didn't smell like me yet. It didn't have the exact right angle of afternoon sun that I'd unconsciously memorized. The walls were too white and my coffee table looked wrong in this room even though it had been right in the last room.

I kept unpacking in stages, like I wasn't fully committing.

## The Rug Incident

The thing that shifted it, embarrassingly, was a rug.

I'd been living with a beige one that came with the apartment and I'd told myself I'd replace it "eventually." Then on a random Wednesday I was on my lunch break and walked into a store and there was a rug that was exactly the color of dried terracotta, slightly faded at the edges, and I bought it without measuring anything.

It fit. Not perfectly — it's about four inches too short on one side — but it fit in the way that matters, which is that it made the room make sense. The couch looked right. The coffee table found its place. It changed the color temperature of the whole room and suddenly I had a space, not just a collection of furniture in a room.

I texted my sister a photo. She sent back: "Oh you live there now."

She was right.

## On Making a Place Yours

I've moved enough times to know that it happens on a different schedule every time, and you can't rush it. Some apartments feel like home in a week. Some take a year. This one took three months and a terracotta rug.

I think what has to happen is: you have to stop comparing it to where you were before. You have to stop looking at the new corners and thinking about the old corners. You have to let the light come through the windows without mourning the different light from a different window.

That's harder than it sounds. We're pattern matchers. We grieve even small losses.

But at some point you start making new associations — this is the spot I always sit in the morning, this is the shelf where my keys live now, this is how the hallway sounds at night when the building settles. You start to know the place on its own terms.

And then, one random Wednesday, you come home and it's just home.

## The White Walls

Still working on it. I'm thinking prints — maybe some drawings, maybe some maps of places I've been. Nothing matching. I want it to feel accumulated, like a life, not designed, like a mood board.

More on that when I figure it out.

There's something about the way light hits old cities that makes you feel like you've stepped inside a memory. Not a clean, well-lit memory — more like one of those half-remembered dreams where everything is slightly amber and you're not sure if you're the visitor or the visited.

Lisbon did that to me.

I woke up at 6:17am without an alarm, which doesn't happen to me anywhere else. The room was still mostly dark but there was this thin stripe of gold pushing through the shutters, and I just lay there for a long time watching dust float through it like it had somewhere important to be.

By the time I made it down to the street, the city was still mid-exhale from the night before. An older man was hosing down the sidewalk in front of his café. A tram — the famous yellow kind, the ones that look like they belong in a picture book — ground its way up the hill making a sound between a creak and a song.

I ordered a coffee I didn't speak the words for. The woman behind the bar understood anyway and brought me a small, perfect espresso with a glass of water I hadn't asked for. I sat at a table with mismatched chairs and watched the city wake up, and felt something I don't feel often enough: like I was exactly where I was supposed to be.

## On Slowing Down

I've been trying to understand why this trip felt different from others. I think it's partly that I went without an agenda. No museum timed-entries, no restaurant reservations. I just had a neighborhood I was staying in and a vague direction each morning.

The strange thing about slowing down is that you start to notice you've been moving fast for a long time — not in a dramatic way, just the low-grade hum of being always slightly ahead of yourself. Always at your phone, always at the next thing.

Lisbon doesn't really let you do that. The hills won't allow it. You can't rush up a 40% grade in flip-flops. You stop. You breathe. You look back down at the city behind you and think, huh.

## What I Brought Home

Not much, physically. A small ceramic tile that cost me four euros from a woman selling them out of a basket near the castle. A recipe for pastéis de nata I'll probably never make but felt important to write down.

But also: a new relationship with mornings. The memory of sitting still in a foreign café and being okay with not understanding everything around me. The feeling that some things are worth moving slowly for.

I've been waking up without an alarm more often since I got back. Not at 6:17 — I'm still myself — but earlier than I used to. I make coffee and don't check my phone right away. Sometimes I watch dust in the light from the window.

It's a small thing. But it came from somewhere real.
